Introducing StreetSense

A road-defect register for a city. Citizens report problems, an AI model helps spot them, a person always decides, crews get ranked repair routes, and everyone can see what is being fixed. This page lists everything the website has today and where to find it.

How a report becomes a repair

Report
A citizen files a street issue with a photo and location, or staff scan an image or upload drive footage.
AI check
A quick photo check rejects pictures that are clearly not roads. The road-defect model then finds defects, draws a box and gives a confidence score.
Human review
A reviewer confirms, corrects or rejects it. Nothing is repaired on AI output alone.
Prioritise
Each confirmed defect gets a condition score, liability flags and a repair deadline.
Work order
A work order is created and grouped into a daily route for a crew.
Repair & proof
The crew uploads before and after photos, and the job closes.
Public update
The public tracker shows status, schedule and totals.

Intelligence desk

Eight tabs:

  • Heatmap & risk: map coloured by severity or predicted severity, a condition score, liability flags, citizen reports waiting for review
  • Review queue: AI detections wait here; uncertain ones first; confirm, correct or reject; confirming creates the work order
  • Prediction: rain, temperature swing and traffic show where roads are likely to get worse
  • Work orders & routes: groups open defects into daily crew routes and creates the orders
  • Equity & budget: spending versus need per zone, ROI, budget allocation
  • Repair verification: before and after photos close the job
  • Metrics & footage: detection share, time-to-repair, cost per km, severity, and drive-video scanning
  • Crew view: a crew's stops in order with GPS, materials and a Navigate link

Getting started

As a citizen

  1. Open the home page and create an account.
  2. Press "Report an issue", describe the problem and add a photo or location.
  3. Scroll to "Know where yours stands" to follow it.

As staff

  1. Sign in at /admin.html with an administrator account.
  2. Open the Intelligence desk. For a demo, press "Load sample Delhi dataset" once.
  3. Check the Review queue, then Work orders & routes.
  4. Use Repair verification when a job is done.

Behind the scenes

  • Everything is stored in a local SQLite database, with photos kept as evidence files and an audit log of staff actions.
  • Accounts use hashed passwords, and sign-ins survive a server restart for 12 hours.
  • Detection uses a YOLO11n road-defect model; images are resized to the size it was trained at first.
  • Maps use Leaflet and OpenStreetMap. Weather comes from Open-Meteo when online.

Honest limits

  • The AI is a helper. Its measured scores (shown under Rules, model and data) are modest, so every detection needs human review.
  • Zone population, income, 311 counts, spending and repair costs are sample placeholders until you add real city data.
  • "Estimated claim exposure avoided" and the cost per km are assumptions, not audited savings.
  • The model can miss defects and mix up similar things, for example a water-filled pothole may be reported as waterlogging. Reviewers correct these, and the corrections are the data for the next training round.
  • The prediction is a rule-based formula, not a model trained on past failures.
  • Road speed, depth and width are estimated unless recorded.
